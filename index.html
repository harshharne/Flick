    Weeks pass. Faces go blank. A blank face doesn't lock.

What I decided

Each one cost me another idea.

The toss picks which, never whether.Dropped a coin that decides if you smoke. Half the time it says yes.
Time opens the pack. Chance doesn't.Dropped a pack that opens only on a lucky toss. That's a slot machine.
The object is the pack.Dropped a die and an app button. The hand already goes to the pack.
Instructions, not warnings.Dropped more information. People have read the pack.
Written on a calm day.Dropped commands from an app. An irritable person resists orders.
Pack for bad minutes, app for calm days.Dropped anything that needs a phone mid-craving.
Faces retire.Dropped a tool you keep forever. It would become the new habit.

Where it could fail

  • A timer might do the same job. Test the pack against a plain four-minute timer.
  • A lock can feel like punishment. It only delays, and you set it yourself. If people pry it open, it failed.
  • A single is easy to buy at a tapri. The lock only covers the pack in your pocket.
  • Standard advice is to throw every cigarette away. Stopping at once beats cutting down, 22% to 15.5%. Flick is for the person who keeps a few anyway.
  • Words and electronics leave people out. Most smokers in India smoke bidis. A printed, picture-only pack comes next.
  • It doesn't touch nicotine. Flick sits beside patches and gum.
